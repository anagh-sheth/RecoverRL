# Grasp choice under a deadline

`grasp_env.py` introduces a separate, harder experiment. Fixed controllers reach for an object and deliver it on a 5×5 grid; the learned policy chooses **fast** or **secure** for each grasp attempt. This isolates sequential grasp decisions. It is not a learned navigation controller or a physical manipulation simulation.

## Task

- Fast costs 1 time unit and fails with an object-specific probability `p`.
- Secure costs 4 units and fails with probability `0.1*p`. This relationship is an explicit toy-model assumption, not a measured robot property.
- Reaching and delivery cost Manhattan distance; release costs another unit.
- Deadline = shortest fast delivery time + scenario slack. A reliable grasp can still miss delivery when time is tight.
- Failure risk stays fixed for the whole episode. Retries never exhaust a scripted fault quota. Random outcomes use a reproducible draw per seed and attempt index, shared across policies at matching attempt indices.
- Reward = verified on-time delivery (1) − 0.01 × elapsed time. There is no privileged distance shaping. Gamma is 1 because elapsed physical time is already charged; differently timed macro actions should not receive the same per-action discount.

The six policy inputs are remaining time, remaining transport cost, available grasp time, fast failures, secure failures, and attempts, with fixed normalization. The policy does not see risk, scenario ID, or seed. Failure counts are observable history that can inform later decisions. Replay ground truth is only for human inspection.

## Frozen protocol

`benchmarks/grasp-v1/LOCK.json` fixes the manifests before evaluation. Every risk/slack cell has equal weight.

| Split | Episodes | Hidden fast risks | Extra time |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Train | 960 | 0.05, 0.30, 0.60, 0.90 | 0, 1, 3, 6 |
| Development | 800 | 0.15, 0.45, 0.75, 0.95 | 0, 1, 3, 6 |
| Reserved final | 800 | 0.20, 0.50, 0.80, 0.98 | 0, 2, 4, 7 |

Seeds and risk values are disjoint across splits; grid locations are not held out. Final-test policies have not been evaluated. Development risk holdout is a modest within-simulator generalization check, not a claim about real objects.

Baselines are always-fast, always-secure, switch-after-failure (secure after a failure only when delivery still fits), and secure-when-feasible. The last baseline prevents using an obviously deadline-unaware strategy as the only comparison.

Each supervised model learns from 2,157 transitions generated by the switch-after-failure teacher on the 960 training scenarios. The teacher receives the same observations as the model. The MLP has two 64-unit hidden layers, six inputs and two outputs; actor/value features are independent. Supervision runs 30 epochs. PPO receives additional training interactions, so any improvement over supervision is a post-training result, not an equal-data comparison. A stronger supervised teacher remains an important control.

Three complete runs use seeds 6, 7 and 8, with independent supervised initialization and PPO sampling. Each runs 100,352 PPO transitions, plus logged critic warmup interactions. We evaluate every final checkpoint, with both greedy and reproducibly sampled actions, on identical development scenarios. No best checkpoint is selected. Wilson intervals describe episode uncertainty for one policy; they do not measure variability across training seeds or paired improvement significance.

## Reproduce

Use the existing frozen files. `freeze` is only for creating a new suite directory and refuses to overwrite one.

```sh
.venv/bin/python grasp_benchmark.py verify
.venv/bin/python grasp_benchmark.py train --seed 7 --steps 100000 --out runs/grasp-reproduction7
.venv/bin/python grasp_benchmark.py evaluate --checkpoint runs/grasp-reproduction7/supervised.pt --checkpoint runs/grasp-reproduction7/ppo.pt --out runs/grasp-reproduction7/dev.json
.venv/bin/python grasp_benchmark.py export --input runs/grasp-reproduction7/dev.json
.venv/bin/python -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Open `http://localhost:8765/grasp.html`. The viewer shows actual macro-action frames, a fixed first scenario per risk/slack cell, and policy scores. It does not animate invented physics. Checkpoints and full raw reports are generated under ignored `runs/`; regenerate them on a fresh clone. Reports contain manifest, source and checkpoint hashes, per-episode outcomes and per-cell summaries. Use a fresh output path for every evaluation.

Final evaluation requires explicit `--split test --final-test` after development/model selection is complete. Loading final manifest metadata for integrity tests does not run a policy on it.

The original seven-action task and composition benchmark are preserved. Their checkpoint shapes differ and they cannot be evaluated directly in this two-action environment. Completion rates across these environments are not directly comparable.
